Plastic pollution in rivers is a growing environmental concern, yet statistical information on the physical properties of riverine litter, such as its mass, size, and shape, remains limited. In this work, we conducted a comprehensive meta-analysis incorporating 13 studies from 11 rivers across four continents, comprising a total of 240,571 litter items classified according to the River-OSPAR litter index.

Using available measurements of litter dimensions and mass, we estimated the joint probability distributions of these variables for different River-OSPAR categories through copula-based modelling. By combining these distributions with the relative occurrence of each litter category, we generated a synthetic dataset representing global patterns of riverine litter. Additional assumptions regarding litter volume and density allowed us to obtain a more complete geometric characterization of the litter items.

The study identifies the 25 most persistent litter types found in riverine environments and provides statistical distributions of their physical properties, including variables relevant to transport modelling such as litter volume, elongation, and flatness. These distributions can support the design of laboratory experiments, numerical modelling, field monitoring, and technologies for the removal of plastic pollution from rivers.
